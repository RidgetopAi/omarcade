Spectrum

The trace is the live mix. What matters here is not one sound's shape but whether each sound still has a band of its own when several land together.

The rally — does the pitch climb read?

§7 asks for the paddle hit to rise with rally length. This plays a whole rally so the climb can be judged, which a single hit cannot show. The rise is PITCH_STEP per hit, capped at PITCH_CAP.

★ Ten balls — the test the plan names

A one-shot must be sized against the mix, not against silence. This fires a realistic ten-ball burst — many brick hits, paddle hits, an item catch — summed into one buffer, so you hear the real constructive interference and the real clipping. If a sound vanishes here, it is too quiet, however good it sounds alone.

Headroom readout. The ear misses clipping on a quiet system; this does not.

Master

Preview volume is not a tuned value and is never exported — it exists so you can listen comfortably. Judge levels with the peak meter, not the knob.

Tuning

Paddle hit

The most-heard sound in the game. Short and clean, or it becomes a nag by level three.

Plain brick

Heard more than anything except the paddle. At ten balls these stack — keep it short.

Reinforced chip (survived a hit)

Must read as "not yet" — duller and lower than a break, or the player cannot hear the difference between damage and destruction.

Reinforced break

The reward for the second hit. Two notes: the chip's pitch, then a fifth above it — the same "that, resolved" shape the racer's checkpoint uses.

Armoured break (the heavy one)

§7 calls this "the heavy one". Weight in a clean-synth palette comes from a low fundamental and a longer tail, not from noise.

Power-up catch

Rising, because catching something good should sound like it.

★ BOMB catch — must sound BAD

§7: "the one place the sound has to carry meaning the visual might miss." The bomb does not glow, so the sound is the only warning. It falls instead of rising, and the upper note is deliberately dissonant — a tritone at 1.41 is the classic wrong-sounding interval.

Magnet catch & release

Two halves of one gesture: the catch holds (a single sustained note), the release lets go (the same note, up and short). The release also fires on the 3s auto-release, so it must not sound like a mistake.

Ball lost

Falls. Should read as a loss without being punishing — it happens three times a game at minimum.

Level clear

Rises and resolves to the octave — the racer's finish proved that is what makes an ending sound finished rather than cut off. It plays under the cascade, so it can afford to be the longest sound in the game.

★ Victory fanfare — level 10

The one piece of actual music in the game: a I–IV–V–I progression, four chords, the last one resolving to the octave and held. That is the most-resolved progression there is, which is why every arcade fanfare since the 70s uses it.

It plays under a held cascade at the end of level 10, so it can be the longest thing in the game — but 70s arcade "music" was fanfares, not songs (Space Invaders was four descending notes). Long enough to feel earned, short enough to hear twice.

Game over

The mirror of level clear: the same shape descending, and it must not resolve. Ending on the unresolved note is what makes it feel like a stop rather than a finish.
